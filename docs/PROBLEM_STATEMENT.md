# VendorIQ — Problem Statement

## The problem
Sourcing teams move from a business requirement to a defensible supplier decision through connected activities: defining requirements, finding potential suppliers, assessing fit, checking evidence quality, preparing RFQs, reviewing quotations, comparing commercial terms, and documenting approvals.

Incomplete or inconsistent information creates risk: unverified claims may be treated as facts, prices may be compared on inconsistent bases, or a low price may obscure a mandatory technical failure.

## Proposed solution
VendorIQ is a sourcing decision-support prototype that organizes these activities into a traceable workflow:
1. Capture a structured sourcing brief and evaluation criteria.
2. Discover and shortlist supplier candidates.
3. Apply deterministic, explainable evaluation scoring.
4. Track provenance and distinguish verified, self-reported, unverified, and missing information.
5. Prepare supplier-specific RFQ documents and route them through human review.
6. Ingest quotations, retain extraction provenance, normalize supported currencies, disclose incomplete landed costs, assess technical compliance, and support documented human decisions.

## Intended users
Procurement and sourcing professionals who need a structured way to organize supplier discovery, evaluation, RFQ preparation, and quotation analysis.

## Value proposition
- **Traceability:** source and provenance information remains visible.
- **Consistency:** deterministic evaluation and transparent comparison logic.
- **Risk awareness:** missing evidence and mandatory technical non-compliance are surfaced.
- **Human control:** approval and award decisions remain with the buyer.
- **Workflow continuity:** sourcing brief, discovery, evaluation, RFQ, and quotation review are connected.

## Scope boundaries
VendorIQ is a prototype, not a supplier certification service or production procurement execution system. It does not independently prove supplier claims, guarantee supplier suitability, automatically send RFQs, create purchase orders, or execute supplier awards. Buyers must independently validate supplier identity, capabilities, certifications, specifications, pricing, and legal/commercial terms.

Demonstration materials must use synthetic quotation examples only. They are illustrative test data, not real supplier offers, market quotations, or endorsements.
